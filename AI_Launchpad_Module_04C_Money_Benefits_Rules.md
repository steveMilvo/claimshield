## Module 04C — Money & Benefits Rules

**Goal:** Understand how earning business income interacts with your Centrelink payment BEFORE you start selling — so you don't accidentally get sanctioned, overpaid, or scared off your own business.

**Intro Video (Seedance Prompt):**
> A participant sits at a kitchen table with a Centrelink letter and a phone. They open Claude and ask a question. The screen shows a clear chart: "$1 earned = $X reported = $Y change in payment." The participant nods, then dials a number on the phone labelled "Benefits Adviser." 15 seconds.

**What to Do:**
1. Identify exactly which Centrelink payment you are on (or aren't).
2. Use AI to understand your income test in plain English.
3. Find out how to report business income properly.
4. Book one free conversation with a benefits adviser before earning a dollar.
5. Sign off with your SW that you understand the rules.

**What You Will Make:** A "Benefits Rules Card" you keep next to your computer — what you're on, what your income test is, how you report — plus a booked conversation with a benefits adviser.

> **🟢 AI Superpower (SLES outcome dimension: financial self-management):** A young person who's never managed their own income reporting can now understand and act on their Centrelink obligations with AI assistance. This skill is foundational — without it, every dollar earned in the curriculum could trigger anxiety, overpayments, or sanctions. With it, every sale is celebrated cleanly.

> **⚠️ Critical limit:** AI is NOT a benefits adviser. AI helps you understand. Final advice about your specific payment comes from a real benefits adviser — and that conversation is part of this module, not optional.

### Open Activity — Get Your Benefits Rules Clear

This activity will take about 60–75 minutes. Take breaks. The conversation with the adviser is a separate appointment, booked through this module.

**Step 1 — What Payment Are You On? (15 min)**

On paper, write down:
- Centrelink payment received: [DSP / Youth Allowance / JobSeeker / None / Other]
- Fortnightly amount: $____
- Are you on **Partial Capacity to Work (PCW)** assessment? [Yes / No / Don't know]
- Are you engaged with a **DES (Disability Employment Services)** provider? [Yes / No]
- If yes — DES provider name: ____________

If you don't know any of these, that's OK. The first task is to find out. Log into your **myGov** account at my.gov.au and check your Centrelink details. If you can't, ask a family member or your SW for help.

> **Safety:** AI never asks for your myGov password, your Centrelink Reference Number (CRN), or your TFN. Anyone asking for these is a scam. Including AI.

**Step 2 — Understand Your Income Test With Claude (20 min)**

Open Claude and paste:

```
I am about to start a small business as part of an NDIS program (AI Launchpad SLES).
Before I earn any money, I need to understand how it affects my Centrelink payment.

My situation:
- Centrelink payment: [DSP / Youth Allowance (Other) / JobSeeker / None]
- Approximate fortnightly amount: $[AMOUNT]
- Partial Capacity to Work assessment: [YES / NO / DON'T KNOW]
- DES provider engaged: [YES / NO]
- I live: [WITH FAMILY / INDEPENDENTLY / SHARED HOUSE]
- I am: [SINGLE / IN A RELATIONSHIP — affects income tests for some payments]

Please explain to me in Year 6 plain Australian English:
1. What is the "income test" for my payment? How much can I earn before my payment
   reduces?
2. How much does my payment reduce for each dollar I earn above the threshold?
3. What counts as "business income" — is it the money I take, or the money the
   business makes overall?
4. How do I report this income to Centrelink (fortnightly? monthly?)?
5. What happens if I forget to report? (Be honest — don't sugar-coat it.)

I am NOT asking for advice. I am asking for education. Tell me to talk to a real
benefits adviser for any decision.
```

Read the answer twice. Don't worry if it's still confusing — the next step is talking to a real person.

**Step 3 — Make Your Benefits Rules Card (15 min)**

On an index card or in a Notes file, write:

```
MY BENEFITS RULES (as of [DATE])
────────────────────────────────
Payment: [DSP / YA / JS / None]
Fortnightly amount before earnings: $____
My personal income test threshold: $____ per fortnight
What I lose for each $1 above threshold: $____
How to report: [Express App / Phone / myGov online]
Reporting frequency: [Fortnightly / Monthly]
My DES provider: [NAME or "None"]
My benefits adviser (booked): [NAME, DATE OF APPOINTMENT]

Rule for me: I will report every sale within [TIMEFRAME].
If unsure: I will call [ADVISER NUMBER] before deciding.
```

Stick the card next to your computer or wherever you'll do business work.

**Step 4 — Book a Free Benefits Adviser Conversation (15 min)**

This is non-negotiable. Free options:

| Service | Who they help | How to reach |
|---|---|---|
| **Welfare Rights Centre** (each state) | Anyone on Centrelink | wrc.org.au — find your state's number |
| **National Welfare Rights Network** | Anyone on Centrelink | nwrn.org.au |
| **Centrelink Disability Officer** | DSP / disability payment recipients | Call 13 27 17 and ask specifically for the Disability Officer |
| **DES provider's case manager** (if you have a DES) | DES participants | Through your existing DES provider |
| **MoneySmart** (ASIC) | General money + benefits questions | moneysmart.gov.au |
| **Financial Counsellor** (free) | Anyone | National Debt Helpline 1800 007 007 |

Call one. Book an appointment. The conversation usually takes 30–60 minutes and is FREE. Bring your Benefits Rules Card.

> **Honesty rule:** Many AI Launchpad participants get blindsided when their first business income shows up on Centrelink. This appointment is the single most important phone call in the whole program. Do not skip it.

**Step 5 — Talk About Reporting Hours, Not Just Money (10 min)**

If you're on Youth Allowance, JobSeeker, or have any mutual obligation requirement:

```
I am on [PAYMENT] with mutual obligation requirements. I'm joining an NDIS SLES
program where I will be running my own micro-business with structured weekly
activities (8-12 hours per week).

Please tell me, in plain Australian English:
1. Can hours spent on a SLES program (or on running my registered business)
   count toward my mutual obligation hours?
2. What's the difference if my SLES program is connected to a DES provider vs
   not?
3. How do I report these hours to Centrelink — through DES, through the
   Express App, through my Job Plan?
4. What's an "approved activity" for mutual obligation, and how do I get
   AI Launchpad classified as one?

I will confirm everything with my benefits adviser — I'm just trying to
understand the structure.
```

Add the answer to your Benefits Rules Card under "Hours reporting."

> **Why this matters:** If AI Launchpad activity counts toward your mutual obligation, you don't need to do separate job-search activities. If it doesn't, you'll be doing both. Knowing which one applies to YOU saves enormous time and stress.

### Billable Touchpoint C — Adviser Conversation Confirmed (15 min, sync voice call)

**NDIS Line Item:** Capacity Building — Skill Development
**This is a HARD SAFETY GATE.** Module 05 does NOT unlock without it.

**To log this billable event:** Type `/submit_mod04c_c`.

1. Voice call AFTER the participant has had their free benefits adviser conversation.
2. The SW asks the participant to explain, in their own words:
   - Which payment they're on
   - What their income test threshold is
   - How they will report business income
   - What hours can count toward mutual obligation (if applicable)
3. If the participant can explain all four, the SW marks them **"Benefits Rules Certified"** and unlocks Module 05.
4. If not — the SW books a second adviser conversation. The module does NOT advance.

> **Why this is the hard gate:** Earning money under SLES is the entire point of the program. Earning money without understanding the benefits interaction has caused real harm to past participants in similar programs (sudden payment cuts, debt notices, sanctions). The hard gate is non-negotiable.

**Quiz Question:**
> When earning your first dollar from a business, what's the MOST important thing to do?
> - A) Spend it on something fun
> - B) Report it to Centrelink within your reporting period — and know how it affects your payment ✓
> - C) Hide it in case it affects your payment
> - D) Ask AI exactly what to do

(The correct answer is B. C is the trap that causes real harm — under-reporting triggers a debt and often a fraud investigation. D is also wrong — AI helps you understand, but reporting decisions need to be confirmed with a real adviser.)
