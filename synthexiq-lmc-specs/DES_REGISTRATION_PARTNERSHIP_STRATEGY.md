# DES Registration & Partnership Strategy
## How AI Launchpad can connect with the Disability Employment Services framework

**Purpose:** Answer five linked questions about Disability Employment Services (DES) and how AI Launchpad SLES delivery should connect to it.

1. How do you become a registered DES provider?
2. Can DES and NDIS be registered under the same company?
3. Where do the paperwork and compliance overlap (and where don't they)?
4. What's the market standard for referral / partnership payments between a SLES program and a DES provider?
5. Can time spent on AI Launchpad each week negate the participant's job-search requirements?

**⚠️ Important caveat:** DES is undergoing major reform. The current contract (DES 2018–2025) is being replaced by the **Specialist Disability Employment Program** (SDEP), with implementation through 2025–2026. Some specifics below reflect the legacy DES framework; the new SDEP framework is still settling. Before committing to a DES registration pathway, **verify against the most recent DEWR (Department of Employment and Workplace Relations) and DSS (Department of Social Services) announcements**. Consult an experienced disability sector consultant for live numbers.

## 1. How to become a registered DES provider

### The structural picture

DES providers are **Commonwealth-funded** (not NDIS-funded). They sit under DEWR / DSS, not the NDIA. Funding flows directly from the Australian Government to the provider — not through participant plans.

Two streams within DES (under the legacy framework):

| Stream | Audience | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **DES-DMS (Disability Management Services)** | People with disability/injury whose work capacity is recovering | Time-limited support to return to work |
| **DES-ESS (Employment Support Services)** | People with permanent disability needing ongoing support | Long-term employment maintenance |

Under SDEP (the new framework), these streams are being consolidated and reshaped. Expect:
- Single licensing framework with tiered specialisation
- Quality-based market entry rather than competitive tender
- Stronger outcome accountability (placement + retention)
- Increased role for specialist providers (autism, intellectual disability, mental health)

### The legacy DES registration path (still relevant for understanding the framework)

Historically, DES has been awarded through periodic **competitive tender rounds** announced by DEWR. Providers bid for **regions (Employment Service Areas)** and **caseload allocations**. Key requirements:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Legal entity** | Australian company, not-for-profit, or government body |
| **National Police Checks** | All staff with participant contact |
| **Working with Vulnerable People** | State-issued, all participant-contact staff |
| **Insurance** | Public Liability ($20M), Professional Indemnity ($5M), Workers Comp |
| **Financial capacity** | Evidence of solvency, working capital, ability to deliver for 12+ months |
| **Quality framework** | National Quality Standards for Employment Services (NQSES) compliance |
| **Operational capacity** | Staff with Cert IV in Career Development / Employment Services or equivalent |
| **Outcome track record** | Either existing track record OR partnership with a track-record provider |
| **Geographic coverage** | Physical office in each Employment Service Area bid for |
| **IT capability** | Integration with **ESS Web** (DEWR's case management system) |
| **Reporting** | Capacity to deliver fortnightly performance data |

### The SDEP / new framework path

The new framework (rolling out 2025-2027) is shifting to a **panel-based licensing model**:

- Application open to providers who meet quality thresholds
- Not competitive against other providers — competitive against the standard
- Smaller providers can enter (less capital required than legacy tender)
- Specialist providers (specific disability types) explicitly supported
- Outcomes auditing through periodic reviews rather than fixed contract terms

**Realistic timeline for AI Launchpad to register independently:** 12–18 months from decision to operational. Significant cost.

**Realistic timeline for AI Launchpad to partner with an existing DES provider:** 4–12 weeks. Much faster, much cheaper, opens almost all the same benefits.

**Recommendation:** Partner first. Register only if the partnership economics break down or the strategic case for owning the DES license becomes clear (Year 2+).

## 2. Can DES and NDIS be registered under the same company?

**Yes.** This is common. The model is well-established.

Examples of providers running both:
- Vision Australia
- Yooralla
- Endeavour Foundation
- MultiCap
- WISE Employment
- ORS Group
- APM (Advanced Personnel Management)
- atWork Australia

### How the dual structure works

| Layer | NDIS side | DES side |
|---|---|---|
| **Parent entity** | One company | One company |
| **Registration / license** | NDIA Provider Registration (NDIS Practice Standards 2021) | DEWR Provider License (under SDEP or legacy DES) |
| **Funding flow** | Through NDIS participant plans | Direct Commonwealth payments |
| **Audit / quality framework** | NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission | DEWR / NQSES |
| **Participant agreements** | NDIS Service Agreement | DES Job Seeker Service Agreement |
| **Staffing** | Support workers, allied health | Employment consultants, job coaches |
| **Often co-located** | Yes, common to share office | Yes |
| **Outcomes measured at** | NDIS plan review | 13-week / 26-week job outcomes |

The key for AI Launchpad: **NDIS and DES are operationally compatible but legally distinct**. Same company, two divisions, two compliance regimes.

### Capacity Building / SLES under NDIS + DES participation under DEWR

A typical AI Launchpad participant on full layered support might have:
- **NDIS SLES funding** — paid to AI Launchpad for the curriculum delivery
- **DES participation** — through AI Launchpad's DES arm OR a partner DES provider, generating Commonwealth outcome payments
- **Centrelink payment** (DSP or YA/JS) — independent of either

If AI Launchpad delivers all three layers (NDIS + DES + Centrelink reporting support), the participant gets a single integrated experience and AI Launchpad captures revenue from two funding streams. This is the strategically strongest position — and also the operationally most complex.

## 3. Where do paperwork and compliance overlap

Significant overlap in the operational basics; minimal overlap in the participant-facing frameworks.

### Operational overlap (can be shared)

| Item | Used for | Cost saved by sharing |
|---|---|---|
| **National Police Check (per staff)** | Both | ~$50/staff/year |
| **Working with Vulnerable People** | Both | $80-200/staff/year (state-dependent) |
| **First Aid certification** | Both | $120-180/staff/year |
| **Public Liability + Professional Indemnity insurance** | Both (different limits) | Bundled premium typically 30-40% cheaper |
| **Workers Compensation** | Both | Single premium |
| **HR system, payroll, finance, IT** | Both | Significant scale efficiencies |
| **Privacy + data protection framework** | Both | Single framework, dual mapping |
| **Safeguarding policies (high-level)** | Both | Single base, supplemented for each |
| **Continuous improvement / quality framework** | Both | Single system, dual reporting |
| **Premises** | Both | Single lease, dual occupancy |
| **Executive leadership + governance** | Both | Single board, dual KPI reporting |

### Compliance distinct (cannot be shared)

| Item | NDIS side | DES side |
|---|---|---|
| **Registration body** | NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission | DEWR / DSS |
| **Quality standards** | NDIS Practice Standards 2021 | NQSES |
| **Audit cycle** | NDIS midcycle + full audit (3 yrs) | DEWR quality reviews + program audits |
| **Participant agreements** | NDIS Service Agreement | DES Job Seeker Service Agreement (or new SDEP equivalent) |
| **Case notes format** | NDIS-aligned, plan-goal-referenced | ESS Web format, outcome-tracked |
| **Incident reporting** | NDIS Reportable Incidents (NDIS QSC) | DEWR Workplace Incident Notification |
| **Pricing / billing** | NDIS Pricing Arrangements (annually updated) | Commonwealth outcome fees (schedule of fees) |
| **Practitioner registration** | Some roles require NDIS worker screening | Some roles require Cert IV Career Development |
| **Quality framework registration** | Approved Quality Auditor (NDIS) | DEWR-appointed quality assessors |

### Practical implication

Running both **costs about 1.4× a single-program operation, not 2×**. The shared infrastructure (HR, IT, finance, premises, insurance, leadership, governance) carries a heavy fixed cost that benefits from dual revenue. But you pay for the specialised teams and audit cycles in each.

For AI Launchpad: **the cost case for dual registration only stacks once you're delivering to ~50+ participants across both programs**. Below that, partner.

## 4. Market standard for referral / partnership payments

**Bottom-line first:** the cleanest economic model is **not** cash-for-referrals (which can trigger anti-inducement concerns under Commonwealth procurement). It's **service co-delivery agreements** that pay AI Launchpad for the value it adds to the participant's DES journey.

### Why cash-for-referrals is a problem

Under the legacy DES framework, providers are paid by DEWR for outcomes (placement, 13-week, 26-week milestones). Paying an external party a flat fee for "sending me a participant" can:
- Distort referral patterns (creates incentive to refer participants who aren't a good fit)
- Trigger procurement compliance review
- Conflict with the participant's right to choose their provider

Most legitimate sector relationships are structured differently.

### The four practical models (legitimate, in use today)

#### Model A — Service Co-Delivery Agreement

AI Launchpad delivers specific activity that the DES provider counts as the participant's plan activity. DES provider pays AI Launchpad a flat fee per participant per day/week of activity delivered.

**Typical rates:** $50–150 per participant per day; $200–600 per participant per week for intensive structured activity.

**Pros:** Clean, transparent, defensible to audit. Both providers retain their own funding streams.
**Cons:** Requires careful documentation per participant per session. Lower upside than outcome-share.

#### Model B — Subcontracted Specialist Activity

DES provider subcontracts a specific activity stream (in our case, the AI Launchpad SLES curriculum) to AI Launchpad. Participant remains on the DES books; AI Launchpad operates as a delivery vendor.

**Typical rate:** 25–40% of the DES funding for the participant's program duration, paid quarterly or monthly.

**Pros:** Predictable revenue. AI Launchpad doesn't carry the DES outcome risk.
**Cons:** Vendor relationship, not partnership. AI Launchpad has less visibility on the participant's full journey.

#### Model C — Outcome Share

AI Launchpad delivers activity (free, or for token co-delivery fee) and receives a percentage of the DES outcome payments WHEN the participant achieves milestones.

**Typical share:** 30–50% of the 26-week outcome payment. The 26-week outcome for high-difficulty participants under legacy DES has typically been ~$10–15k (verify against current schedule of fees).

**Pros:** Aligned incentives. Both providers care about the outcome.
**Cons:** AI Launchpad carries some of the outcome risk. Cashflow is back-loaded (you wait 9+ months for the 26-week payment). Needs strong trust between providers.

#### Model D — Hybrid (most common in practice)

A small per-participant onboarding fee ($100–300) covers admin and initial activity setup; PLUS a co-delivery weekly fee ($200–400 per active participant week); PLUS a 20-30% outcome share on 13-week and 26-week placements.

**Pros:** Cashflow blended (front-loaded admin + middle-loaded activity + back-loaded outcomes). Sustainable.
**Cons:** Complex agreement. Both parties need finance teams able to handle multi-component invoicing.

### What's NOT acceptable

- Flat cash payment for the act of referral (without service delivered)
- Payments structured to disguise referral fees (e.g. "consultancy" with no real consulting)
- Volume-based incentives that reward providers for referrals regardless of fit
- Any payment structure that constrains participant choice

### Recommended position for AI Launchpad

Start with **Model A (Co-Delivery)** in writing with one local DES partner. Move to **Model D (Hybrid)** after 6+ months of co-delivery — you'll have outcome data to negotiate the outcome-share component fairly.

**Typical first agreement target:** $300/participant/week co-delivery for active SLES participants, indexed annually.

For a partner DES provider with 30 referred AI Launchpad participants @ $300/week × 40 active weeks = **$360,000/year** from one DES partnership.

## 5. Can AI Launchpad time negate the participant's job-search requirements?

**Yes — most of the time, for SLES-eligible participants — but it depends on three things.**

### The three conditional dimensions

#### (a) What payment is the participant on?

| Payment | Default mutual obligation | AI Launchpad substitution? |
|---|---|---|
| **DSP** | None | Not applicable — no mutual obligation to negate |
| **Youth Allowance (Other) without PCW** | Full (~20 hr/wk activity, 4 job applications/fortnight) | Possible, with DES Job Plan amendment |
| **Youth Allowance (Other) with PCW** | Reduced (8-15 hr/wk based on capacity) | Highly likely to fully substitute |
| **JobSeeker without PCW** | Full | Possible, with DES Job Plan amendment |
| **JobSeeker with PCW** | Reduced | Highly likely to fully substitute |
| **JobSeeker / YA already on DES** | DES participation IS the activity | Already substituting; AI Launchpad becomes part of DES activity |
| **No income support** | No obligation | Not applicable |

#### (b) Is the activity recognised in the participant's Job Plan?

For mutual obligation purposes, the participant's activity must be:
- Listed in their **Job Plan** (the agreement between participant, DES provider, and Centrelink)
- Classified as an **"approved activity"** (study, work, volunteering, structured employment program, structured self-employment activity)
- **Verifiable** (the provider can report hours to Centrelink)

If AI Launchpad is engaged with the participant's DES provider via a co-delivery agreement (Model A above), the DES provider amends the Job Plan to include AI Launchpad hours as an approved activity. The participant's job-search hours are reduced or eliminated proportionally.

If AI Launchpad is NOT engaged with a DES provider, the participant must either:
- Engage a DES provider themselves and have their consultant amend the Job Plan
- Apply directly to Centrelink for an "approved activity" classification
- Continue meeting full mutual obligations alongside the AI Launchpad curriculum (heaviest burden, sometimes unsustainable)

#### (c) Is the participant participating in DES at all?

If the participant is also engaged with a DES provider:
- The DES engagement satisfies the mutual obligation
- AI Launchpad becomes a co-delivered activity within DES
- Job-search activities are either eliminated or radically reduced
- Reporting is unified through the DES provider

If the participant is NOT engaged with DES:
- Mutual obligation reporting is direct to Centrelink
- AI Launchpad activity can still count, but requires Job Plan classification
- The participant carries more administrative load
- Failure to report correctly can result in payment suspension

### Practical substitution math

A typical YA recipient with PCW might have a mutual obligation of 8 hours/week of approved activity + 4 job applications/fortnight.

If they engage with AI Launchpad delivering 10 hours/week of structured curriculum + business activity:
- The 8-hour mutual obligation is **fully met** by AI Launchpad participation
- The 4 job applications/fortnight may be **reduced or eliminated** if the participant's Job Plan recognises self-employment as the employment outcome path

**Result:** participant focuses entirely on AI Launchpad and their own business. No conventional job-search activity. Payments remain stable. This is the goal.

### What needs to happen for substitution to work

1. **The participant must have DES engagement** (either via AI Launchpad as a DES provider, or via partnership with an existing DES provider). DES is the formal mechanism for substitution.
2. **The DES provider must amend the participant's Job Plan** to:
   - List AI Launchpad as an approved activity
   - Specify the weekly hours of AI Launchpad activity
   - Specify that self-employment / micro-business is the participant's employment outcome path
   - Reduce or eliminate the conventional job-search obligation
3. **AI Launchpad must produce verifiable activity records** (already in scope via the outcomes dashboard and the existing audit-trail-ndis 7-year retention).
4. **The DES provider reports the activity to Centrelink** through ESS Web (or the new SDEP equivalent). The participant doesn't need to do this themselves.

### The integrated experience for the participant

Without DES partnership / substitution:
- AI Launchpad activity in the day
- Plus job-search activities (applications, appointments)
- Plus fortnightly Centrelink reporting of business income
- Plus mutual obligation appointments
- Risk of suspension if any single thread is dropped

With DES partnership / substitution:
- AI Launchpad activity in the day
- DES provider handles reporting
- Self-employment income still reported to Centrelink (legal requirement, can't be substituted away)
- Single point of contact for the participant
- Materially lower administrative burden

This is the strategic case for prioritising DES partnership over independent operation.

## Recommended path forward for AI Launchpad

### Phase 1 — Operational now (next 90 days)

- **Continue NDIS-only operation** for SLES delivery
- **Identify and approach 2–3 local DES providers** for co-delivery partnership conversations
- **Aim to sign one Model A (Co-Delivery) agreement** within 60 days
- **Add Module 04C (Money & Benefits Rules) to the curriculum** as a hard safety gate (already drafted alongside this doc)
- **Brief incoming SLES participants on the DES partnership** at enrolment

### Phase 2 — Partnership operational (months 3–6)

- **First cohort with co-delivery agreement live** — track which participants use DES substitution vs not
- **Build the DES integration spec for the platform** — co-delivery activity reporting via ESS Web or partner DES portal
- **Document the participant journey end-to-end** (intake → NDIS SLES → DES co-delivery → outcomes) — this becomes the partner sales pitch
- **Move toward Model D (Hybrid)** with the partner DES provider — small per-participant fee + co-delivery + outcome share

### Phase 3 — Strategic decision point (month 12)

Three paths forward depending on numbers:

**(a) Stay partnership-only.** If 80%+ of SLES participants are happy with the partner DES experience and the partner is reliable, continue forever. Lowest cost.

**(b) Co-license with the partner.** If the partner is willing, jointly bid for SDEP licensing under a co-branded structure. Shares cost; both keep brand identity.

**(c) Register independently.** If AI Launchpad has 100+ SLES participants and the partner relationship is constraining (slow on amendments, geographic mismatch, etc.), apply for independent DES license.

### Phase 4 — National scale (year 2-3)

With proven SLES outcomes + active DES partnership(s), the platform becomes licensable to other NDIS providers wanting to enter the SLES market. Each licensee then needs their own DES partnership in their region — and AI Launchpad is the matchmaker. This is the white-label scale model from the SLES Pivot Strategy doc.

## Open questions to verify with an experienced sector consultant

Before committing budget to either DES partnership or independent registration, get expert advice on:

1. **Current state of SDEP rollout** — what specific licensing pathway will be in place when AI Launchpad is ready (estimated 2026)?
2. **Outcome payment schedule** — what are the current Commonwealth outcome fees per participant under the new framework?
3. **Geographic catchment rules** — does the new framework still tie providers to specific Employment Service Areas, or is it national?
4. **Specialist provider pathway** — is there a fast-track for providers specifically serving people with intellectual disability, autism, learning disability (AI Launchpad's natural niche)?
5. **Anti-inducement specifics** — current Department position on co-delivery agreements vs. referral fees
6. **Quality assurance for self-employment outcomes** — is the Department's current position that micro-business income meets DES outcome criteria? (This affects the core AI Launchpad value proposition.)

Budget: $500–1,500 for a 2-hour consultation with a specialist disability employment sector consultant. Cheap insurance against committing to a path that the regulator has just moved.
