# AI Launchpad — SLES Pivot Strategy
## Repositioning AI Launchpad as a School Leaver Employment Supports provider

**Status:** Strategic recommendation
**For:** Steve Milverton (MilvoTech / AI Launchpad founder)
**Date prepared:** May 2026

---

## Executive summary

The current hourly Capacity Building billing model produces 10–15% margins at best, and loses money at realistic utilisation. AI Launchpad's curriculum is, by content, a **work-readiness and self-employment program for young people with disability** — which is exactly what NDIS **School Leaver Employment Supports (SLES)** is designed to fund.

Repositioning AI Launchpad as a SLES provider:
- Increases revenue per participant from ~$2k/year (hourly model) to **$22k/year**
- Lifts gross margin from ~10% to **~36%**
- Locks participants into **2-year engagement** (vs the current ~6-month curriculum)
- Aligns NDIS auditing on outcomes (which we can measurably deliver) rather than hourly minutiae

**Recommended path:** pivot AI Launchpad to be SLES-first, with Capacity Building Skill Development as a secondary/post-graduation tier.

## What is SLES

**School Leaver Employment Supports** is an NDIS funding category for young people with disability transitioning from school into employment or income-generating activity.

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Target age | Usually 18–22 (recent school leavers; can extend to 24 in some plans) |
| Funding | Packaged at ~$22,000 per participant per year |
| Duration | Typically 2 years (some 3) |
| Delivery model | Structured, multi-hour-per-week, outcome-measured |
| Outcome expected | Employment, vocational placement, or alternative income-generating activity at exit |
| Reporting | Quarterly progress + annual outcomes audit |

**Critical for AI Launchpad:** SLES outcomes include self-employment and micro-business income. We don't have to fit participants into open employment — we can demonstrate the outcome through their own businesses.

| Money management | Module 10 (Money Basics) + Year 2 deeper modules |
| Communication | Modules 18, 21, 23 |

### Funding model alignment

The hourly model creates a perverse incentive — SWs must hit 99% billable utilisation to break even, which is impossible in practice. SLES's packaged funding model decouples revenue from billable minutes. We get paid to deliver an outcome over 2 years, not to log a precise number of minutes per touchpoint.

### Audience alignment

The current curriculum is designed for **low-literacy, low-numeracy adults with disability** who can run a small business with AI augmentation. This describes a significant chunk of the SLES target demographic — particularly young people with intellectual disability, learning disabilities, or autism who may not fit open employment but can absolutely run their own business with the right scaffolding.

## Positioning — how AI Launchpad sells itself as a SLES provider

### The differentiator (what no other SLES provider can claim)

Most SLES providers focus on traditional employment readiness: mock interviews, work experience placements with employers, customer-service training for retail jobs. The outcomes are real but limited — many participants struggle in open employment for reasons unrelated to skill (sensory environments, social demands, executive function).

AI Launchpad's pitch:

> *"We use AI to set up real businesses your school leaver actually runs themselves. They earn their own income, set their own schedule, learn at their own pace. Self-employment is a legitimate SLES outcome — and for many young people with disability, it's a better one than trying to fit into open employment that wasn't built for them."*

### Three positioning anchors

**1. AI as the equaliser**
Low literacy, low numeracy, executive function challenges — all of these used to disqualify someone from running a business. AI does the writing, the maths, the planning. The participant does the doing.

**2. Real income from Day 90**
By Module 09 (typically 9-12 weeks in), participants have made their first real sale. By end of Year 1, most are earning regular income from their micro-business. This is measurable, demonstrable, and audit-defensible as a SLES outcome.

**3. The business is the workplace**
Customer interactions = employment hours. Sales = work output. Business compliance = workplace responsibility. The participant gets the structure, dignity, and outcome metrics of work — without the barriers of conventional employment.

### Marketing channels

| Channel | Effort | Time to first SLES enrolment |
|---|---|---|
| Direct outreach to SLES coordinators / NDIS planners | High personal effort, high conversion | 4-8 weeks |
| Parent / carer Facebook groups for autism, intellectual disability, school leaver | Medium effort, slow build | 8-16 weeks |
| Allied health network (OTs, speech pathologists working with school leavers) | Low effort, referrals warm | 6-12 weeks |
| School transition coordinators (Year 10-12 special education teachers) | High effort, seasonal | 6-9 months (next school exit cycle) |
| MilvoTech existing customer network | Lowest effort, fast | 2-6 weeks |

**Start with the existing MilvoTech network + allied health.** That gets first revenue in fastest.

## Eligibility gate — who can enrol

Not every NDIS participant fits SLES. Build the eligibility gate into onboarding:

```
Eligibility checklist (all must be true):
[ ] Age 17-22 (negotiable up to 24 with plan justification)
[ ] Has finished or is finishing high school (Year 12 or equivalent)
[ ] Has an NDIS plan with SLES funding allocated, OR
    Has an NDIS planner willing to add SLES funding
[ ] Can engage with structured weekly activities (8-12 hours/week)
[ ] Interested in running their own business (passes a 15-min readiness chat)
[ ] Has a primary support person (family member, guardian, or established SW)
    available for the program
[ ] No active crisis or unmanaged mental health needs that would prevent
    sustained 2-year engagement
```

Anyone failing the gate gets a graceful "not right now" + signposting to alternative supports + invitation to revisit in 6-12 months.

The 15-minute readiness chat covers:
- Does the participant want to run a business, or is this someone else's idea?
- What kind of business are they drawn to? (Don't need a final answer, just genuine interest)
- Can they tolerate the daily AI-assisted workflow (typing or voice OK)?
- What does success look like for them in 2 years?

**Do not enrol anyone who fails the readiness chat regardless of funding availability.** The wasted SLES allocation harms the participant and damages the program's outcome stats.

## NDIA outcome framing — how we defend "running a business = a SLES outcome"

When an NDIA auditor reviews a SLES claim, they want to see evidence of progression toward employment-equivalent outcomes. We document:

### Monthly outcome snapshot (captured automatically)

| Outcome dimension | Evidence we capture |
|---|---|
| Income generated | Total $ in participant's business this month |
| Work activity hours | Sum of customer interactions, business admin, market time, AI-assisted task time (auto-logged) |
| Customer count | Distinct customers served this month |
| Independent task completion ratio | % of activity completed without 1:1 SW assistance |
| Communication interactions | Customer conversations, supplier conversations, community interactions |
| Money management progression | Categorisation accuracy, savings rate, expense awareness |
| Community connections made | New community ties created via Module 11 / 16 |
| Skill progression | Module completion + skill assessment scores |

### Annual outcome report (audit-grade)

Generated each year by an NDIA-compliant report skill. Sections:
- Goals set at intake (from Module 24 or annual planning module)
- Progress against each goal
- Income earned (with substantiation from money tracker)
- Hours of work-equivalent activity
- Independence level changes (pre/post)
- Next year's plan
- Participant + guardian/nominee + SW sign-off

### Defending the "self-employment is employment" framing

If challenged by an auditor:

> "Self-employment is a recognised employment outcome under the NDIS SLES framework. The participant has registered their micro-business [BUSINESS NAME, ABN if applicable], earns regular income from real customers, sets their own work schedule, and engages with the structured AI Launchpad work-readiness curriculum 10+ hours per week. This is documented monthly in the outcomes record. The participant has made measurable progression on every SLES outcome dimension over the reporting period."

Couple this with the existing audit-trail-ndis 7-year retention (already in SynthexIQ) and the NDIA Audit Export Spec — every claim is defensible to the line.

## Partner / licensing model — the multiplier

Once AI Launchpad is delivering SLES outcomes successfully (12-24 months in), license the platform to other NDIS providers.

### White-label SLES delivery package

A licensed partner gets:
- The complete curriculum (04B + 05-24 Year 1 + 25-36 Year 2)
- SynthexIQ integration (their own Telegram bot, their own dashboard)
- The 63-template SW reply library
- Outcomes dashboard + NDIA audit export
- Local SW activation flows
- Alumni network infrastructure
- Updates and curriculum maintenance for the licence period
- Onboarding + train-the-trainer support
- A geography or vertical exclusive (e.g. "exclusive to South Australia regional", or "exclusive to autism specialist providers")

### Pricing

| Partner size | Annual licence |
|---|---|
| Small provider (1-3 SWs, <30 SLES participants) | $30,000 |
| Mid-size (4-10 SWs, 30-100 SLES participants) | $60,000 |
| Large (10+ SWs, 100+ SLES participants) | $100,000 |
| Multi-state / national | $200,000+ |
| Per-participant model (alternative) | $1,500 per active participant/year |

10 mid-size partners @ $60k = **$600k/year recurring** at ~80% gross margin. That's two FTE worth of profit, no SW headcount.

### Sales motion

- Open with case study from your own SLES delivery (after Year 1)
- Target NDIS provider networks (NDS — National Disability Services, state-based provider associations)
- Direct outreach to mid-size NDIS providers (~50-200 staff) — too small to build their own platform, too big to ignore the opportunity
- Annual conference presence (NDS National Conference, Disability Connect events)
- Free pilot for first 3 partners in exchange for case studies + referrals

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| SLES funding rules change | Low (gov't disability funding is stable) | High | Diversify with Capacity Building, alumni recurring, and B2B licensing |
| NDIS auditor rejects self-employment as SLES outcome | Medium | High | Engage NDIA early; reference NDS published guidance on self-employment as outcome; document outcomes rigorously; have legal review of audit defence pack |
| Participant doesn't sustain business beyond program | High (~30-40% normal SLES attrition) | Medium | Strong onboarding gate; outcomes measured against personal goals, not absolute revenue; alumni network sustains engagement |
| Competitor copies the model | Medium | Medium | Curriculum + bot integration is the moat; white-label licensing turns competitors into partners |
| Crisis incident during 2-year engagement | Medium | High | Safety pre-check (Priority 4 in build), on-call SW rota, established escalation to QSC reportable incidents framework |
| Provider can't recruit enough qualified SWs | Medium | High | Train-the-trainer model; recruit at TAFE Certificate III/IV in Disability/Community Services |
| Plan managers refuse to authorise SLES drawdown for self-employment | Low-Medium | High | Build the outcomes framing into the enrolment conversation with plan manager from Day 1 |

## Bottom line

The hourly Capacity Building model **does not pencil**. The SLES model **does** — at a margin (36%) that allows AI Launchpad to fund Year 2 curriculum, scale to multiple SWs, and eventually license the platform.

This is not a small repositioning. It changes who you sell to, how you bill, what outcomes you measure, and how you defend audits. But the curriculum content is already mostly right — we just have to extend it (Year 2), reframe it (employment equivalence), and instrument it (outcomes dashboard).

**Recommendation:** commit to the SLES pivot. Build Year 2 next. Set a Day 60 target of 8-12 SLES enrolments to validate. Adjust from there.
