# White-Label Licensing Package
## How AI Launchpad becomes a platform-as-a-service for other NDIS providers

**For:** AI Launchpad operator (program founder + commercial lead) in Phase 4 of the SLES Pivot Strategy
**Purpose:** Define the complete commercial offering for licensing the AI Launchpad SLES platform to other NDIS / DES providers. This is the **scale lever** — from one operator running 12-48 participants, to a network of licensed partners each running their own caseloads on shared infrastructure.

**Pre-requisite:** AI Launchpad has run at least 4 cohorts (16-20 participants) through to Year 1 completion with measurable outcomes. White-label licensing only becomes credible after the model is proven.

**Target revenue:** $30,000-$200,000 per partner per year, at ~80% gross margin. 10 mid-size partners = $600k recurring revenue.

For partners who prefer pay-per-use:
- $1,500 per active participant per year
- Active = participant who completed at least 1 module in the year
- Minimum: $30,000 (matches Starter floor)
- Maximum: $250,000 (caps at large-network rate)

This model is useful for partners scaling fast and wanting elasticity. Most partners after Year 1 prefer the fixed tier.

## What the partner is responsible for

The licence is platform + curriculum + know-how. The partner brings:

1. **NDIS Provider Registration** in their own name (compliance with NDIS Practice Standards 2021 in their state). AI Launchpad can advise; the partner is the registered provider.
2. **Workforce** — SW recruitment, qualifications, supervision, payroll, training
3. **Participants** — outreach, intake, ongoing relationship
4. **NDIS billing** — invoicing the NDIA / plan managers under their own ABN + Provider ID
5. **DES partnerships** (if pursuing job-search substitution) — negotiated and signed by the partner
6. **Complaints handling** — own incident response, NDIS QSC liaison
7. **Premises / facilities** — physical or virtual; partner's choice
8. **Insurance** — Public Liability, Professional Indemnity, Workers Compensation in the partner's name

This division means partners scale fast (platform is ready) without committing to a single national identity — each retains their local brand and local relationships.

## The sales motion

### Step 1 — Identify the ideal partner

Best-fit partners:
- Existing NDIS provider with 20-200 staff and >100 participants across the organisation
- Some history with school leavers / young adults / capacity building
- Looking to grow SLES delivery but lacks the curriculum/platform infrastructure to do it independently
- Decision-maker is the GM or CEO; not the operational team

Poor-fit partners (politely decline):
- Very large national providers (they'll build their own; not a recurring revenue partner)
- Very small solo practitioners (Starter tier is too much for them; revenue too small for the effort)
- Providers in regulatory trouble or financial difficulty (reputational risk)
- Providers wanting to "white-label" but rebrand all our work as theirs without partnership disclosure (cultural mismatch)

### Step 2 — Build the pitch

Three artifacts go to every prospect:

1. **The one-pager** (PDF, 1 page) — what AI Launchpad is, the SLES economic case, our results, the partnership ask
2. **The 12-slide deck** (PowerPoint or Google Slides) — full pitch: problem, solution, outcomes, partnership structure, ROI, next steps
3. **The demo environment** — read-only access to a sample participant + cohort + outcomes dashboard, so the prospect can see the platform working

The demo environment is the closer. Most prospects sign within 30 days of seeing it.

### Step 3 — Run the pilot

For first 3 partners (the "lighthouse cohort"):
- Pilot terms: 6 months, no licensing fee, half-price onboarding, 5 free participants
- In return: written case study, video testimonial, willingness to host site visits for future prospects, 30-day notice termination if they pull out

After 6 months, pilot converts to standard licensing — most do because they've integrated the platform into their operation and can't easily reverse.

### Step 4 — Onboard at scale

Once 3 lighthouse partners are live:

- Lead conversion: prospects want to talk; pitches close faster
- Case studies: the lighthouse partners speak at conferences and in podcasts (with our support)
- Train-the-trainer: lighthouse partners help train new partner staff (revenue share possible)
- Network effects: partners refer adjacent organisations to us

Target: 10 partners by Year 2 of licensing operation; 20 by Year 3.

## Contract template (Heads of Terms summary)

Full license agreement to be drafted by legal counsel — this is the structural sketch.

```
LICENCE AGREEMENT — HEADS OF TERMS

Parties:
    [LICENSOR LEGAL NAME] (AI Launchpad operator) — "the Licensor"
    [LICENSEE LEGAL NAME] — "the Licensee"

1. Licensed Materials
   - SynthexIQ platform access (instance-per-licensee or shared multi-tenant)
   - LMC Curriculum (full 32 modules + CA pathway)
   - 63 SW Reply Template Library
   - SLES Outcomes Dashboard and reports
   - Weekly Delivery Rhythm + cohort calendar tools
   - NDIA Audit Export
   - SW Caseload Dashboard
   - Intake gate flow (7-stage)
   - Local SW activation workflows
   - Alumni network infrastructure
   - This specs directory (current versions + updates during term)

2. Licence grant
   - Non-exclusive within Licensee's region
   - Region: [STATE / METROPOLITAN AREA / SPECIFIC GEOGRAPHY]
   - Sub-licensing prohibited
   - For Licensee's own SLES delivery only (not for re-licensing or resale)

3. Term
   - Initial: 24 months from effective date
   - Renewal: automatic 12-month rollover unless terminated 90 days prior

4. Fees
   - Onboarding fee: [TIER-DEPENDENT]
   - Annual licence fee: [TIER-DEPENDENT]
   - Custom feature development: at agreed rates, separately invoiced
   - Annual indexing: higher of CPI or NDIS Pricing Arrangements adjustment

5. Brand
   - Licensee operates under its own brand
   - Licensor attribution required in privacy policy + terms of service
     ("powered by AI Launchpad")
   - Co-branding optional at higher tiers

6. NDIS Compliance
   - Licensee is the NDIS Provider of record
   - Licensee owns the NDIS Practice Standards 2021 compliance
   - Licensor maintains the platform's compliance support features
     (audit-trail-ndis 7-year retention, NDIA Audit Export, etc.)
   - Each party owes its own duty of care to its participants

7. Data
   - Licensee owns its own participant data
   - Licensor processes data only for platform delivery purposes
   - Australian data residency maintained (Fly.io Sydney or equivalent)
   - Licensee has full data export capability at any time
   - Termination: 90-day window to export; then deletion per Privacy Act

8. Updates and changes
   - Licensor commits to quarterly platform updates + curriculum maintenance
   - Major curriculum changes (e.g. Year 3 introduction) included if delivered
     in term
   - Breaking changes (rare) communicated 90 days in advance with migration support

9. Termination
   - Licensee can terminate for convenience with 90 days notice (paid through
     remainder of paid period)
   - Licensor can terminate for material breach with 30 days cure period
   - On termination: data export window (90 days), credentials revoked,
     ongoing participants supported by Licensor for current quarter

10. Indemnity + Liability
    - Mutual indemnities for breach of regulatory obligation
    - Indirect/consequential losses excluded
    - Liability capped at 12 months of fees paid

11. Confidentiality
    - Mutual confidentiality of business terms, participant data, partner network
    - Survives termination for 3 years

12. Disputes
    - Australian law, jurisdiction in Licensor's home state
    - Mediation before litigation

[Signatures]
```

## The 30-day onboarding (Starter tier)

For a new Starter partner signed today, here's the standard 30-day onboarding.

### Week 1 — Setup

- [ ] Sign Licence Agreement + insurance certificates exchanged
- [ ] Partner SynthexIQ instance provisioned (instance-per-licensee)
- [ ] Custom subdomain (e.g. `partner-name.synthexiq.com`) configured
- [ ] Brand assets uploaded (logo, colours, copy)
- [ ] Partner admin accounts created (1 program manager, 1 quality lead, 2 SWs)
- [ ] Telegram bot configured with partner's bot token + greeting
- [ ] Currency/locale set (AUD, AEST)

### Week 2 — Training

- [ ] Train-the-trainer: 2-day intensive (10am-4pm both days)
  - Day 1: Curriculum walkthrough; SW reply templates; cohort facilitation; outcome dashboard
  - Day 2: Intake gate; SW dashboard; SW workflows; audit defence
- [ ] Quality team briefed on NDIS Practice Standards 2021 compliance map
- [ ] First test participant added (simulated) to walk through full intake → first module

### Week 3 — Soft launch

- [ ] First real participant intake started (Stage 1)
- [ ] First cohort forming (4 prospects in the intake pipeline)
- [ ] Daily Slack check-in with Licensor support team
- [ ] First minor configuration tweaks based on partner's actual practice

### Week 4 — Live operation

- [ ] First cohort formed and Module 04B unlocked for all members
- [ ] First Touchpoint A submitted by a participant
- [ ] First SW reply sent using a template
- [ ] First billing_events row created and closed
- [ ] First quarterly review scheduled (60 days out)
- [ ] Implementation feedback document submitted by partner

After Week 4 the partner is operationally autonomous. Support transitions to standard email/Slack channel.

## Pricing rationale (don't apologise for the rate)

If a prospect pushes back on the fee, the response:

> *"The licence covers:*
> *— 32-module curriculum that took 18 months to develop and refine*
> *— A bot platform that took 24 months to build, including SLES-specific integrations*
> *— 63 SW reply templates ($300-1000 each to write from scratch)*
> *— Audit-grade outcomes infrastructure that protects $22k/participant of SLES funding from disallowance*
> *— Train-the-trainer for your team*
> *— Quarterly updates including any new modules we develop*
>*
> *At [TIER FEE], you're paying [X]% of one SW's salary for a system that increases each SW's caseload from $98k/year revenue to $264k/year. The fee pays for itself in [N] participants. Most partners break even in month 4-6 of operation."*

The math:
- Starter ($30k/yr): 25 participants × $22k = $550k revenue at standard SLES; net of all costs (SW, manager, overhead, licence) the partner nets $80-120k after 12 months
- Growth ($60k/yr): 60 participants × $22k = $1.32M revenue; net $250-400k after 12 months
- Scale ($100k/yr): 200 participants × $22k = $4.4M revenue; net $1.1-1.6M

ROI is strong. The fee is not the issue; willingness to operationalise the partnership is.

## Multi-partner platform considerations

When AI Launchpad has 3+ active partners, the platform needs multi-tenancy hardening:

| Concern | Solution |
|---|---|
| Data isolation | Each licensee has separate database schema OR strict row-level tenancy on shared schema |
| Configuration per partner | Brand assets, line item rates, curriculum customisations stored per-tenant |
| Cross-tenant analytics for Licensor | Read-only aggregate view available to Licensor staff (with consent in each licence) |
| Migration across partners (e.g. SW joins new partner) | Account portability tools |
| Partner outage isolation | One partner's incident doesn't affect others |
| Per-partner support contracts | Different tiers have different SLAs |

This is engineering work that's NOT in the current Priority 1-5 scope but becomes necessary at Phase 4. Budget: 2-3 engineering months to implement properly.

## Risks specific to the licensing model

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partner damages the AI Launchpad brand through poor delivery | Medium | High | Quality clauses in licence agreement; right to terminate; ongoing observation through platform data |
| Partner uses our platform and then forks/replicates our IP | Low-Medium | Medium | IP clauses in licence agreement; technical safeguards (some features stay Licensor-controlled); legal action if material breach |
| Multiple partners crowd same market | Medium | Medium | Geographic / vertical exclusivity at higher tiers; transparent partner network map |
| Platform engineering can't keep up with partner requests | Medium | High | Cap initial partner count; phased rollout; clear roadmap to partners about what's coming when |
| Partner economics turn out worse than promised | Medium | High | Honest ROI projections, not best-case; pilot partners get cushioning; quarterly business reviews |
| Regulatory change (e.g. SDEP rule change) disrupts model | Medium | High | Watch regulatory changes proactively; share updates with partners promptly; build flexibility into curriculum |
| One partner's complaint escalates to NDIA scrutiny of platform | Low | High | Strong audit defence + transparent communication; protocol for handling NDIA queries about partners |
