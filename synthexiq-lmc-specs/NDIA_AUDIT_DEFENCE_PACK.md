# NDIA Audit Defence Pack
## How to handle an NDIA audit of AI Launchpad SLES delivery

**For:** AI Launchpad operator, program manager, and SW team
**Purpose:** A practical playbook for the moment an NDIA auditor arrives — what to show them, what to say, what to never say, and how to defend the unique "self-employment as SLES outcome" framing.

**When this becomes relevant:** typically 12-18 months after first SLES enrolment, when participants have full annual snapshots and the first plan-review cycle has occurred. Sometimes triggered earlier by complaint, NDIS Practice Standards mid-cycle audit, or random sample.

---

## The three audit scenarios

Different audits ask different questions. Prepare for all three.

| Audit type | Trigger | Focus | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plan Review** | Annual cycle for each participant | Did funded outcomes happen for THIS participant? | 1-2 hours per participant, often by phone or videocall |
| **Practice Standards Audit** | Triennial cycle for all NDIS providers | Are program-wide systems, policies, and quality compliant? | 1-3 days on-site, all paperwork open |
| **Complaint Investigation** | Specific complaint by participant, family, advocate | Did the alleged issue happen? Is it systemic? | Variable — focused on the complaint |

This pack covers all three; the focused scenarios use the same evidence base.

---

## The single most important rule

> **Tell the auditor exactly what happened. Show them the data. Trust the data.**

Do not:
- Embellish outcomes ("she's doing AMAZING")
- Minimise gaps ("she had a tough month, nothing major")
- Speak for the participant ("she would say...")
- Provide opinions in place of evidence

Do:
- Reference the participant's actual monthly snapshot data
- Quote the participant's own words from their monthly reflections
- Show the SW narrative as written at the time, not retrospectively
- Acknowledge gaps honestly and explain remediation

Auditors trust providers who trust their data. Providers who spin their narrative read as defensive — which prompts deeper investigation.

## The "show me how" walkthrough — Plan Review audit

The most common scenario. An NDIA planner reviews the participant's plan to decide if the SLES funding has delivered value and should be renewed (or extended into ongoing Capacity Building post-SLES).

### What the planner asks (typical)

1. *"Did the participant achieve their goals?"*
2. *"What evidence do you have?"*
3. *"How much income did they earn from their business?"*
4. *"How much support did they receive each week?"*
5. *"Is this participant ready for less support / different support / Year 2?"*
6. *"Why should we continue funding this delivery?"*

### How to answer each

**1. *"Did the participant achieve their goals?"***

> *"Here are the 5 goals we set at intake [show Stage 7 service agreement extract + sles_goals]. They achieved 4. The 5th was [SPECIFIC] — they adjusted it at month 6 to [NEW] which they then achieved. Goal-by-goal evidence is in the Annual Outcomes Report — page 3."*

DO have the goals table open and pointable-to. DON'T claim outcomes beyond the data.

**2. *"What evidence do you have?"***

> *"For each goal, we have data from monthly snapshots, the participant's own monthly reflections, SW observations, and the billing event log. May I walk you through the Annual Outcomes Report?"*

Then walk through pages 2-7 of the report. Each page is the answer for one outcome dimension. The page references the underlying data.

**3. *"How much income did they earn?"***

> *"$[X] total this year, evidenced by their money tracker entries. The breakdown by quarter is on page 4. Their income trend goes from $[A] in Q1 to $[B] in Q4 — strong growth."*

DON'T cherrypick months. DO show the full 12-month trend.

**4. *"How much support did they receive each week?"***

> *"Median 9 hours/week of structured activity, split as [X] cohort hours + [Y] 1:1 hours + [Z] self-paced + [W] customer-facing. The breakdown is on page 5. There were 3 reduced-capacity weeks at Month [N] — the SW narrative explains why and how we adapted."*

DO acknowledge the reduced weeks proactively. The auditor will see them in the data; bringing them up shows transparency.

**5. *"Is this participant ready for less / different support?"***

> *"Based on the independence ratio trajectory (from 0.21 at intake to 0.78 at year-end) and the goals achieved, we'd recommend [Year 2 continuation / transition to ongoing Capacity Building / Skill Maintenance + alumni network]. The participant's own preference is documented on page 6 — they said [QUOTE]."*

DO quote the participant directly. DON'T speak for them.

**6. *"Why should we continue funding this delivery?"***

> *"Three things: (a) the data shows continued progression on every outcome dimension; (b) the participant is now earning $[X]/month — that's employment-equivalent income that would not exist without this delivery; (c) Year 2 modules build on Year 1 skills toward sustainability — independence isn't a single endpoint, it's a 2-year build."*

This is where you DO advocate — but always tied to the evidence in front of you.

### What to never say

- *"Trust me"* — auditors never trust a provider's word over data
- *"Self-employment counts as employment because we say so"* — instead: cite NDIA's published self-employment outcome recognition (Schedule of Fees, current SDEP framework guidance)
- *"All our participants do this well"* — averages don't matter; this participant matters
- *"The participant doesn't understand the program"* — implies coercion. NEVER.
- *"We don't have data on that"* — even if true, find data nearby. If genuinely missing, acknowledge and commit to capturing it.

## The "show me your systems" walkthrough — Practice Standards Audit

A program-wide triennial audit. Less about individual participants, more about systems.

### What the auditor will ask to see

| Category | Bring | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Provider registration | Current NDIS QSC Certificate of Registration | Provider's own records |
| Quality assurance | NDIS Practice Standards 2021 compliance map | Quality team's compliance document |
| Workforce | Worker screening checks for all staff, qualification records, training logs | HR system |
| Worker incidents | Incident log per NDIS Reportable Incidents framework | NDIS QSC portal |
| Risk management | Risk register + mitigation actions | Quality team |
| Continuous improvement | CI register + actions completed | Quality team |
| Participant safety | Crisis events log + resolution records | `crisis_events` table |
| Information management | Data handling policy + audit trail + retention | Privacy team + audit-trail-ndis records |
| Service delivery | Service Agreements (sample), Goal records, Outcome reports | Migrations 0016 + 0018 |
| Complaints | Complaints register | NDIS QSC complaint records |
| Cultural safety | Cultural safety policy + training records | HR + DEI team |
| Choice and control | Evidence participants make their own decisions | Touchpoint records + intake readiness conversation summaries |

### How AI Launchpad is unusually well-prepared

Most providers struggle on Information Management (records retention, audit trail) and Service Delivery (evidence of outcomes). AI Launchpad is unusually strong on both:

- **Information Management:** the audit-trail-ndis 7-year retention is automated. Every Touchpoint, every consent, every SW reply has a timestamped record. No paper file gaps.
- **Service Delivery:** the monthly snapshot + annual report system produces NDIA-grade evidence by default, not after the fact.

Use this strength explicitly: *"All Service Agreements are signed digitally and stored in the platform. Here is a sample [click]. All outcomes are captured monthly via the automated snapshot. Here are 12 months of snapshots for participant [X]. The annual report rolls these up."*

Auditors who see this level of system maturity often shorten the audit because they trust the data.

## The "what specifically happened" walkthrough — Complaint Investigation

A complaint has been lodged. The auditor wants to understand exactly what occurred and whether it's a one-off or systemic.

### Step 1 — Don't defend reflexively

The biggest mistake is treating the auditor as an adversary. They're investigating; if you cooperate honestly, the investigation tends to close faster.

### Step 2 — Show the timeline

For any complaint, the platform produces a participant timeline:
- Intake assessment
- Service Agreement
- Monthly snapshots
- Every billing event (Touchpoint, SW reply text, response time)
- Every crisis event and resolution
- Every consent + any withdrawals
- Every SW change or escalation
- Every cohort workshop attended
- Every local SW visit

This timeline is the truth. Show it.

### Step 3 — Acknowledge what went wrong

If the complaint is valid (in part or in whole), acknowledge it:
- "Yes, the participant didn't receive a SW reply for 9 days at Month [N]. That breached our 48-hour SLA. The SW was on leave; coverage failed. We have since [SPECIFIC FIX]."
- "Yes, the participant was upset that [SPECIFIC]. They expressed it in [TOUCHPOINT NOTE]. We responded by [ACTION]. Here is the participant's follow-up on [DATE] saying [QUOTE]."

Acknowledgment + remediation evidence = audit cooperation. Defensiveness + denial = audit expansion.

### Step 4 — Show the systemic check

If the auditor asks "is this happening to others?" — show them:
- The crisis_events table query for similar patterns
- The billing_events SLA breach query for the relevant period
- The continuous improvement register entry for this issue type

If it IS a pattern → acknowledge + show the broader remediation plan
If it ISN'T a pattern → show the query showing it's a one-off + the participant's specific circumstances

## Defending the "self-employment is SLES outcome" framing

This is the unique-to-AI-Launchpad question. Some auditors will challenge it.

### The legal/policy basis

| Source | Wording (paraphrased — verify current) | Why it supports AI Launchpad |
|---|---|---|
| NDIS Act (general purpose section) | NDIS exists to support participants to pursue their goals, including employment, education, social and community participation | Self-employment is a recognised employment outcome under "participate economically and socially" |
| NDIS Operational Guidelines — SLES | SLES funds school leavers' transition to employment, including employment of their choice | "Of their choice" supports self-employment route |
| DEWR SDEP guidance (where applicable) | Recognises self-employment as legitimate employment outcome for participants whose disability impacts traditional employment | Direct support for the framing |
| ATO sole trader framework | A registered ABN holder earning regular income IS engaged in employment (self-employment) | Tax framework recognition |
| Australian Bureau of Statistics employment definition | A person who works for own profit or pays for at least 1 hour/week is "employed" | National statistical framework |

When challenged:

> *"Self-employment is recognised as a legitimate employment outcome by the NDIS Act, the operational guidelines for SLES, the SDEP framework, the ATO, and the ABS. For [PARTICIPANT], the self-employment outcome is documented through the Annual Outcomes Report — including registered business activity, customer transactions, sustained income, and weekly business hours. We treat this with the same rigour as a placement outcome."*

If the auditor pushes harder:

> *"We'd be happy to walk through the Annual Outcomes Report for [PARTICIPANT] showing exactly what employment activity they performed and what evidence supports it. Would that be useful?"*

This shifts from defending a framing to showing evidence. Evidence always wins.

### When the framing has limits

The framing only holds if:
1. The participant is genuinely engaged in business activity (not nominal)
2. There is real income (even if small)
3. There is genuine self-direction (the participant runs the business, not the provider)
4. The activity is documented monthly
5. The participant can describe their own business in their own words

If any of these fail, the framing is weak. Be honest with yourself about which participants can claim self-employment outcome and which need a different SLES exit (such as voluntary work or supported employment progression). The audit will surface participants where the framing doesn't hold; better to identify them yourself first.

## Auditor-visit playbook (the day itself)

### Before the visit (1-2 weeks prior)

1. **Confirm scope in writing** — which audit type, which period, which participants/sample, what evidence the auditor wants
2. **Run the report generation** — pre-build PDFs of every Annual Outcomes Report in scope; pre-export the NDIA Audit Export for the relevant period
3. **Identify the participant cases that might prompt questions** — any reduced-capacity periods, any crisis events, any complaints, any unusual outcomes
4. **Brief the SW team** — who's joining; what they should answer (factually) and what they should defer ("the program manager will speak to that")
5. **Set up the audit room** — physical space, comfortable, all materials accessible, laptop with platform access

### During the visit

1. **Welcome briefly** — introduce who's in the room (program manager, quality lead, possibly the relevant SW)
2. **Walk the auditor through the platform** — 15 min orientation to where everything lives
3. **Open with strength** — pick one outstanding participant case; walk through their Annual Outcomes Report start-to-finish. This sets the standard for the conversation.
4. **Let the auditor ask** — answer factually; refer to data; never speak for the participant
5. **For each question** — find the data; show it; explain it; don't editorialise
6. **For each gap** — acknowledge; show remediation evidence; commit to any further follow-up in writing

### After the visit

1. **Send written follow-up** within 5 business days — recap key questions asked, evidence provided, any actions committed
2. **Action any commitments** — even small ones; auditors note follow-through
3. **Debrief internally** — what worked, what surprised us, what to improve
4. **Update the audit-trail-ndis records** — note the audit occurred, scope, outcome

## Common audit-killing mistakes (don't make these)

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Speak for the participant | Auditor probes deeper; may interview participant directly with sharper questions |
| Round outcomes up | If data shows $4,180 and you say "$4,200", auditor distrusts everything |
| Hide a gap | When found, the audit expands to find what else was hidden |
| Argue back instead of explain | Treated as defensive; audit prolongs |
| Reference policy without showing data | Auditor wants evidence, not principles |
| Claim a participant achieved a goal without documenting how | Treated as fabrication; audit may sample more |
| Cherry-pick which participants to discuss | If 5 are in scope and you only volunteer the best 2, auditor digs into the others harder |
| Read from a script | Auditor wants the manager's actual knowledge, not a pitch |
| Promise corrections you don't follow through | Damages reputation for next audit cycle |
| Treat the SW as the audit target | Auditor sees the SW as the source of truth; defending the SW reflexively suggests cover-up |
