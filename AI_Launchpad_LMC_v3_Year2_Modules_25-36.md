# AI Launchpad — Year 2 Curriculum
## Modules 25–36 — SLES Year 2: Sustain, Grow, Lead, Transition

**For:** AI Launchpad SLES participants entering their second program year
**Builds on:** Year 1 (Modules 04B + 05–24) — graduation in Module 24 marks Year 1 → Year 2 transition
**Duration:** 12 modules over ~40 weeks (one module every 3-4 weeks, slower pace than Year 1)
**Trigger phrases:** `/submit_mod25_a` through `/submit_mod36_c` (36 new touchpoints)

---

## Year 2 design principles

Year 1 was about **starting** a business. Year 2 is about **sustaining and growing** it.

Year 2 modules differ from Year 1 in tone:
1. **More autonomy expected.** Year 1 was scaffolded; Year 2 is supported but participant-driven.
2. **Real money targets.** Each Year 2 module references actual revenue, expense, or income goals.
3. **Employment-equivalent framing.** Every module's AI Superpower sidebar makes the SLES outcome explicit.
4. **Leadership emerges.** From Module 34 onward, participants mentor incoming Year 1 cohorts.
5. **Transition planning.** Year 2 ends knowing what comes next when SLES funding ends.

Touchpoint rhythm stays the same: 3 billable touchpoints per module (A, B, C), each tagged Capacity Building — Skill Development, drawn down from the SLES funding pool.

**Goal:** Use AI to build a full 12-month business plan covering revenue targets, expense budgets, growth milestones, and personal capacity — your roadmap for Year 2.

**Intro Video (Seedance Prompt):**

**What to Do:**
1. Look back at Module 12 (Quarterly Review) and Module 24 (12-Month Plan).
2. Set 3 revenue targets for the year (month 4, month 8, month 12).
3. Use Claude to build the 12-month business plan.
4. Print it. Stick it on the wall.

**What You Will Make:** A printed 12-month business plan + a 60-second video of you reading it (your future self watches this when the year gets hard).

## Module 26 — Repeat Customers

**Goal:** Turn one-time buyers into repeat customers using a follow-up system, loyalty rewards, and an AI-managed customer database — because keeping a customer costs 5x less than getting a new one.

**Intro Video (Seedance Prompt):**
> A participant flips through a small index-card box of customer names. A laptop next to them shows Claude generating personalised follow-up messages. A phone notification chimes: a repeat customer has just placed an order. The participant smiles. 15 seconds.

**What to Do:**
1. Pull every customer name from Year 1's records into one place.
2. Use AI to categorise them (one-time / occasional / regular).
3. Design a simple loyalty reward (free item after X purchases).
4. Send a personalised "we miss you" message to lapsed customers.
5. Set up monthly check-in messages for regulars.

**What You Will Make:** A customer database (Google Sheet or simple Notes file), a loyalty reward system, and a first batch of personalised follow-ups.

> **🟢 AI Superpower (SLES outcome dimension: customer relationship management):** Maintaining customer relationships is core to any service-industry job. Doing it for your own customers with AI assistance is an employment-equivalent skill that translates directly to retail, hospitality, and admin roles. SLES outcome ✅.

---

### Open Activity — Build Your Repeat Customer System

**Step 1 — Pull Customer Names (15 min)**

From your money tracker (Module 10), your social DMs, your phone contacts, your stall receipts — list every customer from Year 1. Aim for 20-50 names.

**Step 2 — Categorise With Claude (15 min)**

```
I have a list of customers from my first year in business. Please help me sort them.

Business: [BUSINESS NAME]
What I sell: [PRODUCT/SERVICE]

Customer list (name + how many times they bought + last purchase date):
[PASTE LIST]

Please sort each customer into:
- REGULAR (3+ purchases, recent within 90 days)
- OCCASIONAL (2-3 purchases over the year)
- ONE-TIME (single purchase)
- LAPSED (any customer with no purchase in 120+ days)

Return as a simple table. Plain English categories. No jargon.
```

**Step 3 — Design Your Loyalty Reward (10 min)**

Pick ONE simple reward you can afford:
- Free [item] after X purchases (e.g. free mug after 5 mugs sold)
- 10% off the next order for anyone who comes back within 30 days
- Birthday discount (collect birthdays)

Tell Claude the structure and ask for a 1-line description for your social bio:
```
My loyalty reward: [DESCRIBE]
Please write a 1-line description I can put in my Instagram bio / on my flyer.
Friendly, clear, no exclamation marks.
```

**Step 4 — Write the "We Miss You" Message (20 min)**

For your LAPSED customers, paste into Claude:
```
Please write a "we miss you" message I can send to a lapsed customer.

Customer's name: [NAME]
Their last purchase: [WHAT, WHEN]
What's new in my business: [1-2 SENTENCES]
What I'm offering: [DISCOUNT / NEW PRODUCT / NOTHING — just hello]

Rules:
- Warm and personal, never desperate.
- Year 6 reading level.
- 4 sentences max.
- Australian English.
- Sign off with my first name.
```

Send the messages over 2-3 days (not all at once).

**Step 5 — Set Up Regular Check-ins (15 min)**

In the bot:
```
/set_customer_checkin [NAME] [FREQUENCY]
```

Example: `/set_customer_checkin Sarah monthly`

The bot reminds you to message that customer at the right cadence. You write the message yourself or use Claude — but the bot keeps you from forgetting.

## Module 27 — Your Money Goals

**Goal:** Use AI to set up business banking, separate personal vs business money, plan for tax, and decide what to do with profit (save, reinvest, or pay yourself).

**Intro Video (Seedance Prompt):**
> A participant opens their banking app on a phone. The screen shows two accounts: "Me" and "Business". A laptop next to them shows a simple table with money flowing in three streams — Save / Grow / Pay. The participant nods. 15 seconds.

**What to Do:**
1. Open a separate bank account just for the business (free options).
2. Use AI to set up a simple 3-bucket money rule.
3. Decide your monthly "pay yourself" amount.
4. Set up basic tax preparation (note: not tax advice).
5. Plan for one small business investment in the next 90 days.

**What You Will Make:** A new business bank account + a 3-bucket money rule + a 90-day investment plan.

> **🟢 AI Superpower (SLES outcome dimension: financial independence):** Managing your own money — separating personal from business, planning for tax, paying yourself a wage — is a recognised employment-equivalent skill and a critical life skill. SLES outcome ✅.

---

### Open Activity — Set Up Your Money System

**Step 1 — Open a Business Bank Account (30 min)**

Most major Australian banks offer free everyday business accounts (ANZ, CBA, NAB, Westpac, ING). You don't need an ABN to open one — you can use it as a "second personal account" labelled "Business" until you register an ABN.

Ask Claude:
```
I am starting Year 2 of running a small business in Australia. I want a separate
bank account just for business income and expenses.

I currently bank with: [YOUR BANK]
I don't have an ABN yet / I do have an ABN: [STATE]

Please tell me:
1. Should I open the business account at the same bank or a different one?
2. What's the cheapest option for someone making <$10,000/year from business?
3. Should I get a debit card linked to the business account? Why or why not?
4. What I'll need to bring/upload to open it (ID, proof of address, etc).

Year 6 reading level. Plain Australian English. No jargon.
```

Visit the bank or apply online. Open the account.

> **Safety:** Never share your account password or PIN. AI does not need this information. Anyone asking is a scam.

**Step 2 — The 3-Bucket Rule (20 min)**

Every dollar that comes into the business splits 3 ways:

| Bucket | % | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **Materials & operations** | 50% | Restocking supplies, replacing what you used |
| **Save / tax / safety net** | 30% | Money you don't touch — for tax time and quiet months |
| **Pay yourself** | 20% | Goes to your personal account as your "wage" |

Adjust the percentages with Claude:
```
I need to split my business income across 3 buckets:
1. Materials & operations
2. Save / tax / safety net
3. Pay myself

My business: [BUSINESS]
My average monthly revenue: $[AMOUNT from tracker]
My monthly materials cost: ~$[ESTIMATE]
My personal weekly money needs (food, transport, fun): $[AMOUNT]

Please:
1. Suggest the right % split for my situation.
2. Tell me when (date in the month) to do the split.
3. Tell me one warning sign that means the split needs to change.

Be honest. I have a disability and a small business. Sustainable > optimal.
```

Write the % split on a card next to your computer.

**Step 3 — Decide Your "Pay Yourself" Amount (15 min)**

Based on Step 2, your monthly pay-yourself amount = (last month's revenue × pay-yourself %).

Set a calendar reminder for the 1st of every month: "Move $[X] from Business to Me".

**Step 4 — Tax Preparation Basics (20 min)**

> **Honesty rule:** This is NOT tax advice. Talk to an accountant before any final tax decision. AI helps you prepare and understand, not decide.

Ask Claude:
```
I am running a small business in Australia. My total business revenue so far this
year is approximately $[AMOUNT]. I do not have an ABN yet / I have an ABN.

I am NOT asking for tax advice. I want to understand what I should be doing to
PREPARE for tax time.

Please tell me, in Year 6 plain Australian English:
1. The $75,000 GST registration threshold — what is it and does it apply to me?
2. The hobby vs business income rule — when does a hobby become taxable?
3. What basic records should I keep (receipts, sales log, etc)?
4. When should I talk to an accountant? (Suggest a revenue threshold.)
5. What does the ATO website (ato.gov.au) have specifically for very small businesses or new sole traders?

Always recommend I speak to a real accountant for any actual decision.
```

Save the answer. Re-read when revenue grows.

**Step 5 — One Small Investment Plan (15 min)**

Pick one thing to invest in over the next 90 days that will grow the business:
- Better camera/lighting for product photos ($50-200)
- Stand-up sign or banner for markets ($60-150)
- A small batch of marketing flyers ($30-80)
- A real domain for your website ($15/year + setup)
- A second product line (variable)

Write down: what, why, cost, when. Save it in the bot:
```
/save_q1_investment [WHAT] [COST] [BY WHEN]
```

## Module 28 — Hiring Your First Helper

**Goal:** Decide if and when to bring a family member, friend, or paid helper into your business — and learn the basics of being someone's employer or work-trade partner.

**Intro Video (Seedance Prompt):**
> A participant and a family member sit at a table sorting product for a market. They look at each other, laugh, and high-five. A clipboard nearby has "Helper Agreement" written at the top. 15 seconds.

**What to Do:**
1. Decide if you actually need a helper right now (or just feel like you should).
2. Pick the kind of helper: family help / friend trade / paid casual / NDIS-paid support.
3. Use AI to draft a simple "what we agree on" document.
4. Set fair pay (or trade) and clear tasks.
5. Have the conversation. Sign the agreement.

**What You Will Make:** A signed "Helper Agreement" + a clear task list for them.

> **🟢 AI Superpower (SLES outcome dimension: leadership and management):** Bringing in help — even one casual helper — is a recognised employment-equivalent skill. Communicating expectations, negotiating fair pay, managing a working relationship: all of these transfer to any supervisory or leadership role. SLES outcome ✅.

---

### Open Activity — Decide, Plan, Agree

**Step 1 — Do You Actually Need Help? (10 min)**

Ask Claude:
```
I am running a small business and considering bringing in one helper.

My business: [BUSINESS]
My average hours per week on the business right now: [HOURS]
What's taking the most time / what I dislike doing: [LIST 1-3 THINGS]
My monthly revenue: $[AMOUNT]

Please honestly tell me:
1. Do I actually need help, or do I just feel overwhelmed and need a break?
2. If help, what kind of help (one task, regular hours, on-call)?
3. What can I genuinely afford to pay or trade for?
4. Is there a risk I'm avoiding the parts of the business that grow it most?

Year 6 plain Australian English. Be honest, not nice.
```

If the answer is "you need a break, not a hire" — take a week off. Come back to this module later.

**Step 2 — Pick the Helper Type (10 min)**

| Type | Best for | Cost | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Family help (unpaid)** | Occasional events (market days, busy weeks) | Free | Resentment if it becomes regular |
| **Friend trade (no cash)** | Mutual support businesses (you help theirs, they help yours) | Free, but real time owed back | Imbalance if one business grows faster |
| **Paid casual** | Specific tasks, peak periods | $25-35/hr cash basis, more if super applies | Tax + employer obligations if regular |
| **NDIS-paid SW (your own SW)** | Help that's part of your AI Launchpad support | Already in your plan | NOT for substituting your own work |

Pick ONE for this module. (Future modules can revisit.)

**Step 3 — Draft the Agreement With Claude (20 min)**

```
I am bringing in a helper for my small business.

Helper relationship: [FAMILY / FRIEND / PAID CASUAL / SW]
Helper's name: [NAME]
What I want them to do: [SPECIFIC TASKS]
How often: [WEEKLY / MARKET DAYS / AS NEEDED]
What I will pay or trade: [DETAIL]
When the agreement starts and ends: [DATES]
What happens if it's not working: [HOW WE STOP]

Please draft a one-page "Helper Agreement" we can both sign.

Rules:
- Year 6 reading level — they need to be able to read it too.
- Warm but clear.
- Includes a "we both can stop at any time with 1 week notice" clause.
- No legal jargon.
- Signatures + date at the bottom.

Australian English.
```

**Step 4 — Set Fair Pay or Trade (10 min)**

Honest fair-pay rules:
- Family help: no cash, but say thank-you with a specific thing (dinner, free product, a thank-you note)
- Paid casual: at least the Fair Work casual minimum (~$30/hr in 2026 — verify current rate at fairwork.gov.au)
- Friend trade: equal hours, written down

> **Honesty rule:** If you pay someone over ~$450/month and it's regular work, you may have employer obligations (tax, super). Talk to an accountant before this becomes regular.

**Step 5 — Have the Conversation. Sign. (20 min)**

Print the agreement. Sit down with the helper. Read it together. If they say yes, both sign.

If they want to change something — change it, reprint, re-sign. The conversation matters more than the document.

## Module 29 — Markets, Pop-ups & Stalls

**Goal:** Run your first real market stall (or your 2nd, 3rd — better than the first) with proper logistics: stock, signage, EFTPOS, market day prep, and a personal energy plan.

**Intro Video (Seedance Prompt):**
> A participant sets up a clean market stall — products arranged on a folding table, hand-lettered price sign, EFTPOS device on a small stand. A customer approaches, smiles, taps the card. The participant gives them a wrapped package and a card. 15 seconds.

**What to Do:**
1. Pick the right market for your product.
2. Plan stock, signage, transport, and EFTPOS.
3. Use AI to write your stall script (greet, pitch, close).
4. Run the market.
5. Debrief honestly within 24 hours.

**What You Will Make:** One run market stall + a debrief document for next time.

> **🟢 AI Superpower (SLES outcome dimension: customer-facing work experience):** Running a market stall is direct, measurable customer-facing work. The setup, sales, social interactions, and end-of-day reconciliation are functionally identical to retail employment — except you're the owner. SLES outcome ✅.

---

### Open Activity — Market Day, End-to-End

**Step 1 — Pick the Right Market (15 min)**

Claude:
```
I want to run a market stall in [YOUR CITY/REGION].

My business: [BUSINESS]
What I sell: [PRODUCT]
My target customer: [FROM MODULE 03]
My budget for stall fee: $[AMOUNT — usually $30-100]
My capacity: I can do [HOW MANY] markets per month

Please:
1. Suggest 3 types of markets that fit me (farmer's market, makers market, community market, weekend market).
2. List 3 actual market names in [REGION] I should research.
3. Tell me what to ask the market organiser before paying any fee.
4. Tell me one red flag that means "this is the wrong market for me."

Year 6 plain Australian English.
```

Pick ONE market for this module.

**Step 2 — The Stock + Logistics Plan (20 min)**

Make a 1-page plan:
- How many products to bring (over-supply for first market — better to come home with some than sell out at 11am)
- How you'll transport them
- Stall furniture (folding table, tablecloth, sign, chair)
- Payment: cash float ($50 in small notes/coins) + EFTPOS device (Square, Tyro Go, CBA Smart Mini — pick one — set it up week before)
- Signage: a clear price list and a hand-lettered sign with your business name
- A snack and water for yourself

**Step 3 — Write the Stall Script With Claude (15 min)**

```
I am running a market stall this Saturday. I want a short, friendly script for
3 moments:

1. GREETING — what to say to someone who pauses at my stall (under 10 words).
2. PITCH — what to say if they pick something up (under 30 words).
3. CLOSE — what to say after they buy (under 15 words).

My business: [BUSINESS]
What I sell: [PRODUCT]
My brand feel (from Module 04): [WARM / NATURAL / etc.]

Rules:
- Year 6 reading level — easy to remember.
- Not pushy. Warm and honest.
- Australian English.
- I have a [DISABILITY type if comfortable disclosing — affects script]. Please
  adjust pace, vocab, and complexity accordingly.
```

Read it out loud 5 times. Memorise the greeting.

**Step 4 — Run the Market (the market day itself)**

Morning checklist:
- [ ] All stock packed
- [ ] EFTPOS device charged and working
- [ ] Cash float in pocket
- [ ] Signage in the car
- [ ] Snacks + water
- [ ] Phone charged

End of day:
- [ ] All money totalled (cash + EFTPOS settled)
- [ ] Stock count remaining
- [ ] Any contact details collected (interested customers, other stallholders)
- [ ] Photos of the stall for social media

**Step 5 — Debrief Within 24 Hours (20 min)**

While the day is fresh, sit down with a pen and write:
- What worked
- What didn't
- One thing to change for next market
- One thing to keep doing
- Total revenue
- Total expenses (stall fee, transport, food)
- Net profit (revenue − expenses)

Add the numbers to your money tracker.

8. **Module 35-36 outcomes meeting** — billing for the planner meeting is complex; may span multiple NDIS line items. SW manually logs each portion via the admin form.
